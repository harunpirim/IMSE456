LO 4-1 · Define the result

Three decisions already made

Week 2: Which project should we do?

Week 3: Where should the project live?

Week 4: What exactly counts as finished?

Following Larson & Gray, Ch. 4

Project scope

The definition of the result or mission delivered to the customer.

It gives the team a boundary for planning and the customer a basis for acceptance.

Developed with the project manager, customer and significant stakeholders.

The scope statement checklist

  1. Project objective
  2. Product scope description
  3. Justification
  4. Deliverables
  5. Milestones
  6. Technical requirements
  7. Limits and exclusions
  8. Acceptance criteria

An objective that governs a plan

Deliver what, by when, within what cost, and at what location if location matters.

Plan and deliver a two-day manufacturing conference for 500 registered participants in Fargo by 15 October, at a total project cost not exceeding $180,000.

Four terms that get blurred

What it is
Deliverable An output
Milestone A recognizable control point
Requirement A performance or technical constraint
Acceptance criterion Evidence that permits acceptance

The omission that becomes a dispute

Limits and exclusions name what the project will not deliver.

Silence is not a boundary. It is room for two stakeholders to hold different assumptions.

Scope, SOW and charter

Scope statement / SOW: What exactly has been authorized?

Project charter: May the project start, and may the manager form the team?

LO 4-2 · Control scope creep

Scope creep

The gradual expansion of requirements, specifications or priorities after work begins.

A change can improve a project.

It becomes creep when its consequences are not deliberately accepted with it.

Following Larson & Gray, Ch. 4

Six common causes

Poor requirements analysis “I will know it when I see it”
Users involved too late Real needs arrive after design hardens
Complexity underestimated Unknown interfaces appear
Too many decision-makers Compromise adds features
No change control Requests enter without a trade-off
Gold plating The team adds unrequested value

Gold plating is not free quality

It spends somebody else’s budget.

It may make acceptance harder by changing the result the customer actually requested.

A controlled change has a path

Record request → analyse impact → authorize decision → update scope and plan together

Impact means time, cost, performance and risk.

Decision means approve, reject or defer.

LO 4-3 · Decide which constraint wins

The project trade space

Redrawn from Larson & Gray, Ch. 4, Figure 4.1

Three priority labels

Meaning
Constrain The original target is fixed
Enhance Use opportunities to improve it
Accept It may move when a trade-off is unavoidable

One possible conference decision

Time Performance Cost
Constrain ●
Enhance ●
Accept ●

Hold the date. Improve participant value. Allow approved contingency to absorb trade-offs.

A matrix has to discriminate

Marking all three constrain means the manager has no priorities.

Marking all three enhance is a wish list.

The matrix matters when it tells the manager what to protect first.

LO 4-4 · Work breakdown structure

From scope to manageable work

Redrawn from Larson & Gray, Ch. 4, Figures 4.3 and 4.4

The whole has to survive the breakdown

The project is the sum of its lower-level elements.

If the children do not add up to the parent, work is missing or counted twice.

Different branches may stop at different depths.

A work package answers five questions

What? Defined work
How long? Duration and dates
Cost? Time-phased budget
How much? Resources
Who? One responsible person

Why the WBS is a database

Schedule, budget, resources, responsibility and performance all attach to the same work-package code.

The diagram is the visible index. The management data beneath it is the point.

Coding position into identity

1       Project
1.1     Major deliverable
1.1.1   Subdeliverable
1.1.1.1 Work package

Everything beginning 1.2.3 belongs to the same branch and can be reported together.

LO 4-5 · Organization breakdown structure

Two structures, two questions

WBS: What has to be delivered?

OBS: Which organization unit is accountable?

Their intersection is a cost account — a control point joining work and responsibility.

Following Larson & Gray, Ch. 4

The conference intersection

Work package Accountable unit
Confirm keynote speakers Program committee
Configure registration Information systems
Prepare venue rooms Operations
Launch campaign Marketing

Now the manager can ask whether the work is late, whether the unit is over budget, or both.

LO 4-6 · Process breakdown structure

When components are the wrong first question

Some projects become knowable through a sequence of phases.

Each phase changes what the next phase can do.

A PBS makes that process the hierarchy.

Following Larson & Gray, Ch. 4

A software PBS

Redrawn from Larson & Gray, Ch. 4, Figure 4.6

Use a PBS when

  • phase completion is the natural control point;
  • outputs from one phase become inputs to the next;
  • the work is a repeatable process; and
  • a component-first breakdown would be artificial.

A project can combine phases, deliverables and work packages.

Small work may need only one grid

Tasks in rows. People in columns.

Following Larson & Gray, Ch. 4

The row test

One unmistakable owner.

The matrix is compact. Duration, cost and acceptance still have to live somewhere.

LO 4-8 · Communication plan

Not a list of meetings

For each information need:

  1. What is collected?
  2. Who receives it?
  3. When?
  4. By what method?
  5. Who may access it?
  6. Who provides it?

Following Larson & Gray, Ch. 4

Power and interest

Redrawn from Larson & Gray, Ch. 4, Figure 4.9

An operating table

Information Audience When Method Provider
Milestone report Sponsor, PM Biweekly Dashboard Project office
Status and decisions Team, sponsor Weekly Meeting record PM
Escalated issue Affected leads At threshold Meeting + record Issue owner
Approved change Team, vendors As approved Change log Change authority

The missing column

Everybody expects information.

Nobody owns producing it.

The provider column prevents that failure. Access limits prevent a different one.

The chain

  1. Scope defines the result.
  2. Priorities settle trade-offs.
  3. WBS or PBS decomposes the work.
  4. OBS or responsibility matrix assigns it.
  5. Codes and cost accounts support control.
  6. The communication plan moves the information.

Busy without this chain is not the same as controlled.

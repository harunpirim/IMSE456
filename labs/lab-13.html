Week page
Week 13 — Being an Effective Project Manager · Managing Project Teams

This is individual work about your team’s project. Each member maps the partner’s network on their own; comparing maps afterwards is useful for your team, but what you upload is yours. Use roles, not names, for people in the partner organization (“operations manager”, not a person’s name), and leave out anything your partner asked you to keep confidential, in Smartsheet and above all in what you paste into the model. The course assumes a Gemini Pro account; if you cannot get one, see the instructor or TA rather than paying for it yourself.

The week page mapped the conference’s network: twelve stakeholders, what the project needs from each, and where each sits on the power/interest grid. Today you build the same register in Smartsheet, check it against the conference, then do it for real for the organization your final project is working with. You do the mapping yourself first. Only then do you ask a model, and what is graded is the reconciliation: what it found that you missed, what it got wrong, and what you changed your mind about.

Part 1 · Build the register on the conference — 12 minutes

  1. Import. Download the conference stakeholder register. In Smartsheet, + Create → Import → Microsoft Excel; keep row 1 as headers and Stakeholder as the primary column. Check: twelve rows, Sponsor to Caterer.

  2. Make Stance a dropdown. Right-click the Stance header → Edit Column Properties → Dropdown (Single Select), with the values Supporter, Indifferent, Antagonist, in that order. Do the same for We need, with Cooperation, Approval, Could block.

  3. Two formula columns. Add two Text/Number columns, Priority and Strategy. In row 1 of each paste the formula below, then right-click the cell → Convert to Column Formula. A score of 3 or more counts as high.

    Column Formula
    Priority =[Power]@row * [Interest]@row
    Strategy =IF(AND([Power]@row >= 3, [Interest]@row >= 3), "Manage closely", IF([Power]@row >= 3, "Keep satisfied", IF([Interest]@row >= 3, "Keep informed", "Monitor")))

  4. Check. Sort by Priority, descending (right-click the header → Sort Rows). Check: Sponsor 25 is first and Program chair 15 second; four rows read Manage closely, four Keep satisfied, three Keep informed, one Monitor. If yours differ, fix the formula before going on.

  5. Card View. Switch to Card View, View by: Stance. In the card settings show Strategy, We need and Currency on each card.

  6. Play the case. In the Tuesday case the IT director refused to release Priya. Drag the IT director’s card to Antagonist. Screenshot 1: the Card View board after the move.

Part 2 · Your partner’s network, unaided — 18 minutes

No AI in this part. The point is to find out what you see.

  1. Copy the sheet. File → Save as New, name it FP stakeholder register, and open the copy. Delete rows 2 to 12. Overwrite row 1 with your first stakeholder; the formula columns fill in for every row you add.
  2. Add a Source column. A Dropdown column Source with the values Me, Model. Every row you enter in this part is Me.
  3. List the stakeholders. Ask the chapter’s three questions about your project: whose cooperation do we need, whose approval, whose opposition could stop us? Enter at least ten stakeholders, from at least five of the groups in Figure 1 on the week page: your team, the partner contact, the contact’s boss, the people who do the work you are improving, IT or facilities, suppliers, regulators, the instructor and TA as top management of the course, and so on. Fill in every column. Score power and interest honestly; a partner contact who never answers email has low interest whatever their title.
  4. Draw the map. On paper, draw your project in the middle and every stakeholder around it, in the style of Figure 2 on the week page: a dashed line for cooperation, a solid line for approval, a heavy or red line for could block. Photograph it.
  5. Screenshot 2: the register in Grid View, sorted by Priority descending, with every column visible.

Part 3 · Bring in the model, then reconcile — 12 minutes

  1. Open Gemini Pro. Describe your project in three sentences, without confidential details, and paste this after it:

    Using the three questions "whose cooperation will we need, whose agreement
or approval will we need, whose opposition could stop us", list every
stakeholder this project depends on. For each, give a power and an interest
score from 1 to 5, what the project needs from them, and what they are likely
to value in return. Include stakeholders a student team would be likely to miss.

  2. Compare its list with yours, line by line. Build a three-part reconciliation table in your PDF:

    Stakeholder Why
    The model found, I missed, I accept why they matter to your project
    The model found, I reject why they don’t apply, or what it got wrong
    I found, the model missed what you know that it couldn’t

  3. Add every stakeholder you accepted to your sheet with Source = Model, and re-sort by Priority.

If the model found nothing you missed and got nothing wrong, say so; that is a finding, not a failure. Do not invent an error to fill the table.

Part 4 · Influence plan — 8 minutes

For the three stakeholders at the top of your re-sorted register, fill in a table in your PDF:

Stakeholder What we need from them before FP4 Currency we can offer (week page, LO 10-3) Next contact: who, how, when

Part 5 · Answer — 10 minutes

Questions 5 and 6 are about your own final-project team, not the partner.

  1. Conference. After you moved the IT director to Antagonist, the card shows Keep satisfied and Approval. Why is that combination the one most likely to surprise a project manager, and what should the coordinator have done about it before the corridor conversation?
  2. Your map. Which stakeholder on your map has the most power and the least interest? What would happen to your project if they said no, and when did you last hear from them?
  3. Reconciliation. How many stakeholders did the model add that you accepted, and how many did you reject? Which single addition or error matters most to your project, and why?
  4. Influence. For the top stakeholder in your influence plan, why that currency and not another? What does it cost your team to offer it?
  5. Your team’s stage. Which of the five stages of team development is your final-project team in now? Give one piece of evidence from the last two weeks, and name two of the nine situational conditions from LO 11-3 that your team lacks.
  6. One norm. Propose one norm for your team’s last three weeks, in a sentence the whole team could sign, and say which pitfall or conflict it is meant to prevent.

Upload — 5 minutes

The week page’s practice problems add a communication-channels calculation, a power/interest classification and three conflict scenarios, if you want more practice after class.

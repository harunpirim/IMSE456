Week 5 — Estimating Project Times and Costs

This is individual work. Gemini can challenge an assumption or propose a second method. You remain responsible for the units, formulas, evidence and recommendation.

The project is a two-day manufacturing conference. A top-down sponsor decision set an $80,000 cap. The starting bottom-up estimate is $82,511. The difference is intentionally small enough that weak reasoning in either direction can change the decision.

The goal is not to force the estimate below the cap. The goal is to produce a more defensible estimate and state what the sponsor should do next.

Part 1 · Import and reconstruct the hierarchy — 15 minutes

Download the Week 5 conference estimating workbook.

  1. In Smartsheet, select + Create → Import → Microsoft Excel.
  2. Select the workbook and import the Smartsheet Import worksheet.
  3. Keep Task Name as the primary column.
  4. Set WBS and Parent WBS to Text/Number so codes such as 1.2.1 remain intact.
  5. Use Indent Level and Parent WBS to reconstruct the hierarchy:
    • level 0 is the project;
    • level 1 rows sit beneath the project; and
    • level 2 rows sit beneath the matching level 1 parent.
  6. Collapse each parent and expand it again. Confirm that the expected work packages move with it.

Do not alter estimates yet. First preserve the starting case so later changes remain visible.

Part 2 · Add Smartsheet formulas — 15 minutes

The import sheet contains calculated values because Excel and Smartsheet use different formula syntax. Open the workbook’s Formula Guide worksheet and recreate these calculations as Smartsheet column formulas.

Target column Leaf-row formula
Duration Range (days) =[High Duration (days)]@row - [Low Duration (days)]@row
Labor Cost =[Planned Effort (hours)]@row * [Labor Rate]@row
Total Estimate =([Labor Cost]@row + [Nonlabor Cost]@row) * (1 + [Contingency %]@row)

For additive fields on parent rows, use =SUM(CHILDREN()). Apply it to:

  • Planned Effort (hours);
  • Labor Cost;
  • Nonlabor Cost; and
  • Total Estimate.

Run three checks.

  1. Change one leaf-row labor rate by $1. Confirm that Labor Cost, Total Estimate and every affected parent update.
  2. Restore the original rate.
  3. Compare the project total with the starting value $82,511. If it does not reconcile, locate the first parent where the rollup differs.

Format labor rate, labor cost, nonlabor cost and total estimate as currency. Format contingency as a percentage.

Part 3 · Challenge the evidence — 20 minutes

Filter Risk Level to High. Read the range, method and assumption for each high-risk work package.

Improve at least three assumptions. Each revised assumption must contain one of the following:

  • a measurable condition;
  • a named source;
  • a validity date;
  • a capacity or quantity limit; or
  • a threshold that would trigger re-estimation.

Weak:

Speakers should respond quickly.

Stronger:

Twelve invited speakers will accept or decline by 6 October; re-estimate travel and schedule effort if fewer than eight accept.

Use Gemini to challenge one assumption. Paste this prompt, replacing the bracketed fields.

Act as an independent estimator. Challenge this project assumption:

[assumption]

The associated work package is:
[work package, scope, low/likely/high duration, cost, and method]

Identify missing conditions, quantities, evidence, and expiration dates.
Propose a more testable assumption. Do not change the estimate and do not
claim access to historical data that I have not provided.

Record the original assumption, the model’s challenge and your final wording. Reject any suggested evidence that cannot be verified.

Part 4 · Re-estimate one high-risk package — 20 minutes

Choose one high-risk work package and estimate it with a second method.

Acceptable pairs include:

  • expert judgment and Delphi;
  • ratio and bottom-up detail;
  • template and parametric;
  • point estimate and low/likely/high range; or
  • internal estimate and an external supplier quote.

Document the second method in a new column or attached row note. Include:

  1. the method;
  2. the source or calculation;
  3. the units;
  4. the result;
  5. the reason the two estimates differ; and
  6. the value you chose for the revised plan.

Update the Smartsheet row only after documenting the comparison. Do not average two estimates merely because they differ. Select the value supported by the stronger evidence and explain the decision.

Part 5 · Make the cap decision — 10 minutes

Compare the revised bottom-up total with the $80,000 sponsor cap. Write a recommendation of no more than 100 words.

Choose one action:

  • accept the revised estimate and approve additional funding;
  • reduce or change defined scope;
  • obtain better evidence before commitment; or
  • retain the cap because the revised estimate supports it.

Your recommendation must state:

  • the revised total and variance from the cap;
  • the work package that contributes the most decision-relevant uncertainty;
  • the evidence that changed your view; and
  • the next authorized action.

A smaller number is not automatically a better estimate. A decision with visible evidence is stronger than an unsupported total that happens to fit the cap.

Part 6 · Save and upload

Check the submission package.

Save the Smartsheet export, screenshot and recommendation in IMSE456/studio-05/ on your NDSU Google Drive or OneDrive.

Upload one PDF to Blackboard before you leave the session. Put the recommendation first, followed by the evidence screenshot, second-method comparison and AI acknowledgement. Name it SA5_yourlastname.pdf.

If Smartsheet is unavailable, complete the calculations and evidence challenge in Excel. State which hierarchy and formula checks could not be performed, preserve the file, and complete those checks after access is restored.

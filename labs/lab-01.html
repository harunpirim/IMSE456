Week page
Week 1 — Modern Project Management

This is individual work. Everyone produces their own page. The final project is where you work in a team; the studio is where the thinking gets into your own hands.

Part 1 · Build — answer first, unaided

You go first. The model goes second. That order is the whole point, and reversing it destroys the only data this studio produces.

Open the five review questions on the week page. Close every other tab.

  1. Answer all five from the reading and the Tuesday session. Rough notes are fine — full sentences are not required yet.
  2. Where you are unsure, write “unsure” and move on. Do not guess to fill the space.
  3. Where you cannot answer at all, write “no answer”.

Keep this draft. You are going to compare against it, and you cannot reconstruct it afterwards.

Part 2 · Use — now bring the model in

  1. Give the model the same five questions. Ask for its answers.
  2. For every question — including the ones you were confident about — ask it one follow-up that pushes on something specific: “which of the five characteristics does that example actually fail?”, “give me a counter-example”, “what would change your answer?”
  3. Keep the transcript, or the link to it. You need it for the acknowledgement.

Part 3 · Reconcile, and write the page

This is the graded part. Everything before it was raw material.

For each of the five questions, decide what you now believe and write that — your answer, not the model’s, and not a merge of the two performed without deciding.

Then account for the difference. On the page, name:

  • At least one place the model was wrong, incomplete, or confidently vague. Say what was wrong with it. “It was generic” is not specific enough; “it defined a program as any set of projects and dropped the common-goal requirement, which is what separates a program from a portfolio” is.
  • At least one place it changed your mind, and what specifically moved you.

Part 4 · Save it, then upload it

Check this first:

Then two steps, in this order. They are not the same step, and skipping the first is how people lose a semester of work.

1 · Save your own copy to the cloud. Use the Google Drive or OneDrive that comes with your NDSU account — both are provided to you by the university and neither costs anything. Make a folder IMSE456/studio-01/ and save the file there as SA1_yourlastname.pdf.

This copy is yours. It stays with you after the semester ends, and it is what you go back to when you want to show someone what you built.

Never save course work only to a local disk. Lab machines wipe on reboot, laptops get left at home, and a drive that fails the night before a deadline is not an extension.

2 · Upload the PDF to Blackboard before you leave the session. That copy is what gets graded, and you will get written feedback on it.

Study loop for this week

Run this before you read the notes, and again after. It is not graded; it is the fastest way to find out what you have wrong.

Be my tutor on the difference between a project and an operation. Ask me
one question at a time. Don't lecture. Start by making me state the test,
then hand me cases that break it — a film studio, a release train, a
refinery shutdown — until I stop treating it as a fact about the world.

Week 7 — Managing Risk

This is individual work. Parts 1 to 3 are done without AI. Part 4 brings the model in. What is graded most heavily is Part 4: your judgement about where the model’s solution and yours differ, and which one is right.

Tuesday ended with a probability: an 84% chance the conference opens by day 32. This studio teaches the method behind that number from the beginning, on the chapter’s own example, and then asks you to apply it to a problem you have not seen worked. Use the PERT section of the Week 7 notes as your reference. Four formulas are all you need:

Formula Use it for
Expected time \(t_e = (a + 4m + b)/6\) one activity
Variance \(\sigma^2 = ((b - a)/6)^2\) one activity
Path standard deviation \(\sigma_{T_E} = \sqrt{\sum \sigma^2}\) the activities on one path
Standard score \(Z = (T_S - T_E)/\sigma_{T_E}\) one path and one date

Download the Week 7 PERT studio workbook. It has four sheets: Worked example, Exercise 1, Z table and Reconciliation. Fill the yellow cells. Enter formulas, not typed numbers, so that a changed estimate flows through.

Part 1 · Learn the method on the chapter’s example — 25 minutes

The Worked example sheet holds the six activities of the chapter’s hypothetical network, with predecessors and three estimates each. We work through it together on the board; you build the same thing in the sheet as we go.

  1. Expected time. Enter \(t_e\) for each activity. Check: the weight of 4 sits on the most likely time, so \(t_e\) is pulled only a little toward the long tail.
  2. Variance. Enter \(\sigma^2\) for each activity. Notice which activities are uncertain and which are nearly fixed.
  3. The network. Sketch it on paper from the predecessor column. Run a forward pass on the \(t_e\) values and name the critical path.
  4. Path spread. Add the variances of the critical activities, then take the square root. Never add standard deviations.
  5. A date. For \(T_S = 67\), compute \(Z\) and read the probability from the Z table sheet or with =NORM.S.DIST(Z, TRUE). Then do the same for \(T_S = 60\).

Your sheet should agree with the chapter: \(T_E = 64\), \(\sigma_{T_E} = 6\), and about a 69% chance of finishing by 67 and a 25% chance by 60 [1]. If it does not, find the cell that disagrees before moving on. The usual culprit is a standard deviation added where a variance belonged.

Part 2 · Exercise 1 on your own — 30 minutes

The Exercise 1 sheet holds the chapter’s first appendix exercise, reproduced as written [1]:

Given the project information in the following table, what is the probability of completing the National Holiday Toy project in 93 time units?

Exercise 1 from Larson & Gray, Ch. 7, Appendix 7.1 [1].
Act. ID Description Predecessor Optm. (a) Most likely (m) Pess. (b)
1 Design package None 6 12 24
2 Design product 1 16 19 28
3 Build package 1 4 7 10
4 Secure patent 2 24 27 36
5 Build product 2 17 29 47
6 Paint 3, 4, 5 4 7 10
7 Test market 6 13 16 19

Work in the same order as Part 1.

  1. \(t_e\) and \(\sigma^2\) for all seven activities.
  2. The network, sketched on paper. Activity 6 is a merge with three predecessors; write down all three candidate early finishes before choosing.
  3. The forward pass on \(t_e\), and the critical path. Mark the critical activities in the Critical column.
  4. \(T_E\), the sum of the critical variances, and \(\sigma_{T_E}\).
  5. \(Z\) for \(T_S = 93\) and the probability of finishing by 93.

Then complete the Paths block: the sheet lists every complete path through the network. Fill in each path’s sum of \(t_e\), sum of variances and \(\sigma\). You will need them in Part 3.

Part 3 · Interpret the number — 10 minutes

Answer three questions in the Exercise 1 sheet, in no more than 150 words in total.

  • Which path other than the critical one is closest in expected length, and how does its variance compare with the critical path’s?
  • The chapter’s probability looks only at the critical path. Is the true chance of finishing by 93 higher or lower than that figure? Explain using the merge at activity 6.
  • What scheduled time would give the sponsor 90% confidence? Use the critical path and \(Z = 1.28\), and say how many time units of buffer that adds over \(T_E\).

Part 4 · Let the model solve it, then argue — 15 minutes

Give Gemini the exercise cold, without your answer. Use your NDSU Gemini Pro account; the free tier can run out partway through a studio. If you cannot get access, see the instructor or TA rather than paying for an account.

Solve this PERT problem and show every step: expected times, variances,
the network and forward pass, the critical path, the project expected
duration, the path standard deviation, Z and the probability.

Given the project information in the following table, what is the
probability of completing the National Holiday Toy project in 93 time
units?

[paste the seven-row table from the Exercise 1 sheet]

Then state one thing the single-path probability leaves out.

In the Reconciliation sheet, compare its solution with yours line by line: each \(t_e\), each variance, the critical path, \(T_E\), \(\sigma_{T_E}\), \(Z\), the probability, and its answer to the last prompt. For every difference, say which of you is right and why. Correct your sheet if the model found a real error; record the model’s error if it made one. Models often add standard deviations instead of variances, or run the forward pass on \(m\) instead of \(t_e\).

Keep the reconciliation to one page. If the two solutions agree on every line, say so in the first row and describe the two checks you made to be sure. That is a finding, not a failure.
